# Getting Started with NASA Space Apps

## What is the NASA Space Apps Challenge?

The NASA Space Apps Challenge is an international hackathon in which participants work in teams to address challenges using open data from NASA and its space-agency partners. Projects can take many forms: websites, apps, visualizations, educational resources, research concepts, hardware prototypes, stories, and more.

You do not need to be an expert programmer. Teams benefit from different skills, including research, science, design, writing, communication, and presentation.

## A beginner's roadmap

1. **Learn about the event.** Read the current year's official website and participant terms.
2. **Create an account and register.** Follow the official registration instructions and deadlines.
3. **Choose an event format.** Check available local events and virtual participation options.
4. **Find teammates.** Look for people with complementary skills and agree on communication.
5. **Explore the challenges.** Read the full challenge descriptions and supporting resources.
6. **Understand the problem.** Identify who is affected, what is known, and what data could help.
7. **Plan a small solution.** Decide what you can realistically build during the event.
8. **Build and test.** Keep the solution focused; document assumptions and limitations.
9. **Prepare the presentation.** Explain the problem, your approach, the data, the result, and what comes next.
10. **Submit through the official platform.** Follow the current year's instructions exactly.

## What skills can I contribute?

- Programming and software development
- UI/UX and visual design
- Science, geography, and research
- Data analysis and visualization
- Writing, storytelling, and education
- Project coordination and presentation

## Before the event

- Read the official rules and schedule.
- Check internet, laptop, power, and software access.
- Agree on team communication and task tracking.
- Make a simple plan and decide how you will make decisions.
- Keep a record of data sources, licenses, and contributors.

## During the event

- Re-read the challenge statement before building.
- Start with a small working prototype.
- Check that the data actually supports your idea.
- Share progress often and ask for feedback.
- Reserve time for testing, documentation, and submission.

## Important

This guide is independent and general. Event dates, team limits, eligibility, local-event arrangements, submission requirements, and judging criteria may change. The current official website and participant terms are authoritative.
