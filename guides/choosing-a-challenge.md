# Choosing a Challenge

Choosing a challenge is about finding a meaningful problem that your team can understand and address with the time and skills available.

## A practical selection process

### 1. Read the full challenge statement

Do not choose based only on the title. Read the problem description, goals, expected deliverables, judging information, and linked resources on the official challenge page.

### 2. Identify the problem

Write down:
- What problem is being addressed?
- Who or what is affected?
- Why does the problem matter?
- What would a useful improvement look like?

### 3. Explore the data

Open the suggested datasets and resources. Check:
- What does each dataset measure?
- What time period and geographic area does it cover?
- What format is it in?
- Are there missing values or limitations?
- Can your team access and understand it?

### 4. Match the challenge to your team

Consider your team's interests, skills, available tools, and time. A challenge that excites the team and has accessible data may be a better fit than one that sounds impressive but is difficult to investigate.

### 5. Define a small, testable idea

Write one sentence describing the user, problem, and proposed solution. Then list the minimum features needed to demonstrate it.

## Avoid common mistakes

- Picking a challenge before reading its full description.
- Assuming a dataset contains information it does not contain.
- Trying to solve an entire global problem in one weekend.
- Building features before understanding the intended user.
- Treating a prototype as a fully validated scientific tool.
- Forgetting to document data sources and limitations.

## Final check

Before committing, make sure every team member can explain the problem, the data you plan to use, and the first version of the solution.
